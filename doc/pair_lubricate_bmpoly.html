pair_style lubricate/bmpoly command

Syntax

pair_style lubricate/bmpoly mu flaglog flagfld cutinner cutoff [flagHI flagVF]
pair_coeff I J [cutinner cutoff]

All arguments are numeric. The optional flagHI and flagVF arguments default to 1. The two cutoff values are surface-to-surface gaps, not centre-to-centre distances.

ArgumentMeaning
muDynamic viscosity of the suspending fluid. It is positive and is expressed in the current LIGGGHTS unit system; for units si, use Pa s.
flaglog0 omits the logarithmic resistance terms; 1 includes them. The normal inverse-gap term is retained in both modes.
flagfld0 disables background-fluid drag, rotational drag, and stresslet; 1 enables them.
cutinnerPositive minimum gap used in the resistance law. Gaps below this value, including overlap, are clamped to this value.
cutoffPositive maximum surface gap for pairwise lubrication. Pairs with a larger gap are skipped.
flagHI0 disables pairwise hydrodynamic interactions; 1 evaluates the pair resistance law. If flaglog=1, the implementation forces this flag to 1 because the logarithmic terms require the pair interaction.
flagVF0 uses the dilute background coefficients; 1 applies the volume-fraction correction to the background coefficients.
I JAtom-type pair for pair_coeff. The optional pair-specific cutinner cutoff overrides the global values.

Description

lubricate/bmpoly implements the unequal-sphere short-range hydrodynamic resistance model described by O. Cheal and C. Ness, Rheology of dense granular suspensions under extensional flow, arXiv:1801.03805v3. It is a lubrication contribution for spherical particles in a Newtonian suspending fluid. It does not replace a granular contact law.

Geometry and notation

The following definitions apply to one ordered pair, called particles 1 and 2. The implementation evaluates the same relation over a full neighbour list with newton off.

SymbolDefinition
a1, a2Radii of particles 1 and 2. They must be positive.
r1, r2Particle-centre position vectors.
r = r1 - r2Separation vector, pointing from particle 2 to particle 1.
d = |r|Centre-to-centre separation.
h = d - a1 - a2Physical surface-to-surface gap before clamping.
hmodelGap used by the model: max(h,cutinner), provided h < cutoff.
n = r/dUnit vector from particle 2 to particle 1.
xi = 2 hmodel/(a1+a2)Dimensionless gap used in the asymptotic resistance functions.
beta = a2/a1Radius ratio. The formulas are valid for unequal spheres and reduce to the equal-radius case when beta=1.
U1, U2Translational particle velocities.
Omega1, Omega2Angular particle velocities.
DeltaU = U2-U1Relative translational velocity used by the pair resistance law.
I3 by 3 identity tensor.
n otimes nDyadic product of n with itself.
P = I - n otimes nProjector onto the plane normal to n.
lnNatural logarithm. The code uses L = ln(1/xi) when flaglog=1.

The neighbour-list centre-distance cutoff is the selected surface cutoff plus the radii of the two types. Consequently, cutinner and cutoff should be chosen in length units and should be small compared with the particle radii. A pair is evaluated only when its physical gap is below cutoff; its gap is then clamped before evaluating xi.

Pairwise force and torque

With the coefficient definitions below, the coefficients already contain mu. The force and torque on particle 1 are therefore:

F1h = XA11 n (n . DeltaU) + YA11 P . DeltaU
      + YB11 (Omega1 x n) + YB21 (Omega2 x n)

G1h = YB11 (DeltaU x n)
      - P . (YC11 Omega1 + YC12 Omega2)

F1h is the hydrodynamic force vector, G1h is the hydrodynamic torque vector, . is tensor-vector or dot contraction, and x is the three-dimensional cross product. The corresponding contribution for particle 2 is evaluated by the full-list traversal with the particle labels exchanged.

Resistance coefficients

Let L=ln(1/xi) when flaglog=1, and L=0 when flaglog=0. The six unequal-sphere Bidisperse-Monodisperse polynomial coefficients used by the implementation are:

CoefficientDefinition
XA116 pi mu a1 [ 2 beta^2/((1+beta)^3 xi) + beta(1+7 beta+beta^2)L/(5(1+beta)^3) ]
YA116 pi mu a1 [ 4 beta(2+beta+2 beta^2)L/(15(1+beta)^3) ]
YB11-4 pi mu a1^2 [ beta(4+beta)L/(5(1+beta)^2) ]
YB21-4 pi mu a2^2 [ beta^(-1)(4+beta^(-1))L/(5(1+beta^(-1))^2) ]
YC118 pi mu a1^3 [ 2 beta L/(5(1+beta)) ]
YC128 pi mu a1^3 [ beta L/(10(1+beta)) ]

pi is the mathematical constant. The dimensions follow from the powers of radius: the XA11 and YA11 terms multiply a velocity, the YB terms couple rotation and translation, and the YC terms multiply angular velocity to produce torque. In particular, YC12 uses beta, not beta^2.

Background-fluid field

When flagfld=1, an isolated particle also receives background-fluid resistance. For particle i with radius ai:

Fi^fld = -R0 ai (Ui - Uinf)
Gi^fld = -RT0 ai^3 (Omegai - Omegainf)
Si^fld = -RS0 ai^3 Einf

Here Uinf is the local background-fluid translational velocity, Omegainf is its local angular velocity, Einf is its symmetric rate-of- strain tensor, and Si^fld is the stresslet contribution tallied in the stress/virial calculation. In a fixed, undeformed box these field values are zero. With fix deform ... remap v, they are obtained from the imposed box deformation.

R0, RT0, and RS0 already include mu. Their volume-fraction correction is controlled by phi:

volP = sum_i (4 pi ai^3 / 3)
volT = available box or supported wall-enclosed volume
phi  = volP/volT when flagVF=1, otherwise 0

flaglog = 0:
  R0  = 6 pi mu (1 + 2.16 phi)
  RT0 = 8 pi mu
  RS0 = (20/3) pi mu (1 + 3.33 phi + 2.80 phi^2)

flaglog = 1:
  R0  = 6 pi mu (1 + 2.725 phi - 6.583 phi^2)
  RT0 = 8 pi mu (1 + 0.749 phi - 2.469 phi^2)
  RS0 = (20/3) pi mu (1 + 3.64 phi - 6.95 phi^2)

volP is the total particle volume and volT is the available fluid volume. The optional volume calculation recognizes the legacy fix wall interface; granular wall fixes are not used for this correction.

Numerical and units notes

  • The model is a short-range asymptotic resistance law. It is not a finite-gap fluid solver.
  • cutinner regularizes the singular inverse-gap resistance. It is a numerical/model cutoff, not a physical contact distance.
  • The equations above are written in physical current-unit form; the implementation applies LIGGGHTS' normal internal unit-conversion factor when accumulating forces, torques, and stress.
  • The pair style applies no Hertz, Hooke, friction, collision, or overlap-restoring force. Use pair_style hybrid/overlay when a granular contact model must be combined with lubrication.
  • Because the style uses a full neighbour list and evaluates ghost velocities, use newton off and communicate single vel yes.
  • If the simulation can create overlap, choose a mechanical contact model and timestep independently of the lubrication cutoff. The lubrication clamp alone does not prevent overlap.

Restrictions

The pair style requires a spherical atom style such as atom_style granular or atom_style sphere with positive radii, angular velocities, and torques. Use:

newton off
communicate single vel yes

If fix deform is used, it must use remap v. Only one legacy fix wall command is supported for the finite-volume correction.
