:line

pair_style lubricate/bmpoly command :h3

[Syntax:]

pair_style lubricate/bmpoly mu flaglog flagfld cutinner cutoff &
  [flagHI flagVF] :pre

pair_coeff I J [cutinner cutoff] :pre

mu = suspending-fluid dynamic viscosity :ul
flaglog = include logarithmic resistance terms (0 or 1)
flagfld = include background-fluid drag, rotation drag, and stresslet (0 or 1)
cutinner = minimum surface-to-surface gap used in the resistance law
cutoff = maximum surface-to-surface gap for lubrication interactions
flagHI = include pairwise hydrodynamic interactions (0 or 1)
flagVF = apply the finite-volume-fraction correction to background drag (0 or 1) :ul

The optional {flagHI} and {flagVF} values default to 1.  The command requires
0 < {cutinner} <= {cutoff}.  The cutoff values for this style are gaps between
surfaces.  Internally, the neighbour-list cutoff is the selected gap plus the
two particle radii.

[Examples:]

pair_style lubricate/bmpoly 0.001 1 1 1.0e-6 5.0e-5 1 1
pair_coeff * * :pre

See `examples/PICI-LIGGGHTS/lubricate_bmpoly/` for a runnable lubrication-only
case and its `granular_plus_lubrication/` subdirectory for a
`hybrid/overlay` example.

[Description:]

Style {lubricate/bmpoly} implements the unequal-sphere resistance functions
given by Cheal and Ness, "Rheology of dense granular suspensions under
extensional flow", arXiv:1801.03805v3.  It is a short-range resistance model
for spherical particles in a Newtonian suspending fluid.

For one ordered pair, particles 1 and 2 have radii {a1} and {a2}, centre
positions {r1} and {r2}, translational velocities {U1} and {U2}, and angular
velocities {Omega1} and {Omega2}.  The geometric variables are

{r} = {r1} - {r2}
{d} = |{r}|
{h} = {d} - {a1} - {a2}
{hmodel} = max({h},{cutinner})
{n} = {r}/{d}
{xi} = 2 {hmodel}/({a1}+{a2})
{beta} = {a2}/{a1}
{DeltaU} = {U2}-{U1}
{I} = identity tensor
{P} = {I} - {n} otimes {n}
{L} = log(1/{xi}) when {flaglog}=1, otherwise 0 :pre

Here {h} is the physical surface-to-surface gap.  A pair is skipped when
{h} >= {cutoff}; otherwise {hmodel} is used in the resistance law.  Thus an
overlap is not a contact force: it is merely evaluated at the regularising
gap {cutinner}.  The vector {n} points from particle 2 to particle 1, {I} is
the 3 by 3 identity, {n} otimes {n} is a dyadic product, and {P} projects
onto the plane normal to {n}.  The dot denotes contraction, the cross denotes
the three-dimensional cross product, and {log} is the natural logarithm.

The coefficients below include {mu}; therefore the pair force and torque on
particle 1 are

F1h = XA11 {n} ({n} . {DeltaU}) + YA11 {P} . {DeltaU}
      + YB11 ({Omega1} x {n}) + YB21 ({Omega2} x {n})

Gamma1h = YB11 ({DeltaU} x {n})
          - {P} . (YC11 {Omega1} + YC12 {Omega2}) :pre

The six unequal-sphere Bidisperse-Monodisperse polynomial coefficients are

XA11 = 6 pi {mu} {a1} [ 2 {beta}^2/((1+{beta})^3 {xi})
       + {beta}(1+7 {beta}+{beta}^2) {L}/(5(1+{beta})^3) ]

YA11 = 6 pi {mu} {a1} [ 4 {beta}(2+{beta}+2 {beta}^2) {L}
       /(15(1+{beta})^3) ]

YB11 = -4 pi {mu} {a1}^2 [ {beta}(4+{beta}) {L}
       /(5(1+{beta})^2) ]

YB21 = -4 pi {mu} {a2}^2 [ {beta}^-1(4+{beta}^-1) {L}
       /(5(1+{beta}^-1)^2) ]

YC11 = 8 pi {mu} {a1}^3 [ 2 {beta} {L}/(5(1+{beta})) ]

YC12 = 8 pi {mu} {a1}^3 [ {beta} {L}/(10(1+{beta})) ] :pre

Here {pi} is the mathematical constant and {mu} is the fluid dynamic
viscosity.  When {flaglog}=0, {L}=0, so all logarithmic terms vanish while
the inverse-{xi} term in {XA11} remains.  The cross-rotation coefficient
{YC12} uses {beta}, not {beta}^2.

When {flagfld}=1, particle {i} with radius {ai} also receives background-field
terms:

Fifld = -R0 {ai} ({Ui}-{Uinf})
Gammaifld = -RT0 {ai}^3 ({Omegai}-{Omegainf})
Sifld = -RS0 {ai}^3 {Einf} :pre

{Uinf} is the local background-fluid velocity, {Omegainf} is its local angular
velocity, {Einf} is the symmetric rate-of-strain tensor, and {Sifld} is the
stresslet contribution tallied in the stress/virial calculation.  These field
values are zero in a fixed undeformed box.  With {fix deform remap v}, they are
obtained from the imposed box deformation.

The background coefficients already include {mu}.  Let

{volP} = sum_i 4 pi {ai}^3/3
{volT} = available box or supported legacy-wall volume
{phi} = {volP}/{volT} when {flagVF}=1, otherwise 0 :pre

For {flaglog}=0:

R0 = 6 pi {mu} (1 + 2.16 {phi})
RT0 = 8 pi {mu}
RS0 = (20/3) pi {mu} (1 + 3.33 {phi} + 2.80 {phi}^2) :pre

For {flaglog}=1:

R0 = 6 pi {mu} (1 + 2.725 {phi} - 6.583 {phi}^2)
RT0 = 8 pi {mu} (1 + 0.749 {phi} - 2.469 {phi}^2)
RS0 = (20/3) pi {mu} (1 + 3.64 {phi} - 6.95 {phi}^2) :pre

The pair style uses the current LIGGGHTS unit system.  In {units si}, {mu}
is in Pa s, radii and gaps are in metres, velocities are in metres per second,
and angular velocities are in radians per second.  The same dimensional
relationships apply in other unit systems.  The implementation applies the
normal LIGGGHTS internal unit-conversion factor when accumulating forces,
torques, and stress.

This style calculates hydrodynamic forces and torques only.  It does not
provide a mechanical contact force, friction law, or collision regularisation.
If the simulation can bring particles into overlap, use a validated mechanical
contact model in addition to this hydrodynamic contribution.

The model is a short-range asymptotic resistance law.  The paper uses a
minimum gap of 0.001 particle radii and neglects interactions beyond about
0.05 particle radii.  Choose {cutinner} and {cutoff} accordingly and do not
interpret the style as a general finite-gap hydrodynamics model.  The pair
style supplies no Hertz, Hooke, friction, collision, or overlap-restoring
force.  Use {pair_style hybrid/overlay} to combine it with a granular contact
model when overlap is possible.

:line

[Mixing, shift, table, tail correction, restart, rRESPA info:]

For unset type pairs, the inner and outer gap cutoffs are mixed geometrically.
The pair style writes its settings and per-type-pair gap cutoffs to binary
restart files.  Shift, table, and tail corrections are not relevant.  The
style uses a full neighbour list and is not an rRESPA pair-style component.

:line

[Restrictions:]

The pair style requires {atom_style granular} or another spherical atom style
with positive per-atom radii, angular velocity, and torque storage.  Use

newton off
communicate single vel yes :pre

If {fix deform} is used, it must use the {remap v} option.  Only the legacy
{fix wall} interface is recognized for the finite-volume correction; granular
wall fixes are not cast to that interface.
